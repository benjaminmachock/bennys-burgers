Our Story

Built in a foundry.
Cooked like one.

Benny opened the doors in 2019 inside a converted iron foundry at the edge of Highlands Ranch, Colorado, tired of burgers that were more bread than beef. The idea was simple: fewer ingredients, hotter griddle, more attention to the two minutes that actually matter.

Every patty is hand-portioned from fresh 80/20 chuck — never frozen, never pre-formed — and smashed thin enough that the whole surface hits the flat-top at once. That's what gets you the crackling, deep-brown crust before the inside overcooks. No fillers, no shortcuts, nothing on the bun that doesn't earn its spot.

The Technique

How we smash it

A smashburger isn't just a thin burger — it's a different cooking method that changes the flavor entirely. Here's the two-minute process, start to finish.

01

Portion & press

A 3oz ball of fresh chuck hits the 450°F flat-top and gets smashed flat with a steel weight in one motion — not pre-formed into a puck, never pressed twice.

02

Sear hard, sear fast

Smashing thins the patty and roughly doubles its surface contact with the griddle, so the sugars and proteins caramelize into a dark, craggy crust in under 90 seconds — the Maillard reaction, maxed out.

03

Stack & serve

Cheese melts directly onto the patty while it's still on the heat, then it's built on a toasted potato bun and out the pass while the crust is still crackling.

Why not just a regular burger? A thick patty cooks slowly from the outside in, so by the time the center is done, the surface has barely browned. Smashing trades thickness for surface area — more crust, more char, more flavor in every bite, and a shorter cook time that keeps the inside juicy instead of gray.
